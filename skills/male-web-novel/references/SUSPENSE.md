# Suspense Brain Holes (悬疑脑洞)

## Overview
Stories set in parallel worlds with ghosts, monsters, or supernatural threats. Protagonist typically has system/cheat ability. Settings include death games, rule horror, ghost recovery, apocalyptic horror.

## Key Elements
- **Background**: Modern world with ghosts/monsters + supernatural rules
- **Core Tone**: Tense, dangerous, mystery-driven, survival-critical
- **Characteristic**: High stakes (death), strong mystery, logic puzzles or horror suspense

## Common Subgenres

### Death Game/Thriller Game (惊悚游戏)
**Core**: Real-world humans are selected for high-mortality horror/danger games

**Setup Examples**:
- "Real world has mysterious app; selected users enter death games"
- "Strangers wake up in locked room, must play deadly games to escape"
- "Competitive elimination: survivors advance, losers die"

**Game Types**:
- **Horror Hospital/Asylum**: Qingshan Psychiatric Hospital-type settings
- **Death School**: Students in school survival scenario
- **Urban Survival**: City with supernatural monsters hunting specific targets
- **Team vs. Team**: Multiple sides in survival competition

**Cheat Examples**:
- "Scan ghost fear/resistance to exploit weakness"
- "See death game's true win conditions behind facade"
- "System granting supernatural tools for each level"
- "Analyze/decode cryptic game rules"

**Appeal**: Fast-paced, tight logic puzzles, survival horror, intense conflicts

### Rule Horror/Rule Weird Tales (规则怪谈)
**Core**: Selected humans enter high-mortality scenarios governed by strict rules

**Core Mechanic**:
- Players receive detailed survival rules (written, spoken, or environment clues)
- BUT: Some rules are **fake**, contradictory, or incomplete
- Must use logic, observation, and experimentation to distinguish real vs. fake rules
- Breaking rules = death or terrible consequences

**Example Setup**:
```
Given Rules:
1. Don't open closet after midnight
2. If you see red eyes, run immediately
3. The doctor's office is safe (always trust it)
4. When someone dies, cover their eyes; if you uncover, they resurrect as ghost
...
Reality: Rule 3 is fake; the doctor is the real danger. Rule 1 is also misleading.
```

**Protagonist Strategies**:
- Test rules carefully (risking life to learn truth)
- Find contradictions to deduce which are fake
- Exploit hidden loopholes or misunderstandings
- Work with/trust/distrust other players

**Cheat Examples**:
- "See true rules directly; distinguish fake rules with different colors"
- "Predict what happens before following a rule"
- "Modify or bypass rules temporarily"
- "Communicate with ghost survivors who know truth"

**Core Satisfaction Loop**:
Observe rules → Logic/puzzle solve → Deduce true rules → Survive level → Reveal backstory behind world/system

**Appeal**: High-intelligence puzzles, logical reasoning, gradual world-building, layered mysteries

### Ghost Recovery (诡异复苏)
**Core**: Realsim: Spiritual energy (灵气) and ghosts/monsters recover simultaneously. Humanity gains cultivation power but faces monstrous threats; war between human faction and ghost faction.

**World Setup**:
- Before: Ordinary world, no ghosts
- Event: "Great Recover" (大复苏) happens — both spiritual energy and ghosts return
- Now: Humans can cultivate (gain power) BUT must fight ghosts to survive

**Power Dynamics**:
- **Human side**: Cultivators, government organizations, powerful families, sects
- **Ghost side**: Different ghost types (spirit, entity, curse, monster)
- **Conflict**: Territory, resources (spiritual stones, cultivation sites), survival

**Protagonist Cheat Examples**:
- "Kill ghost → absorb ghost power to upgrade"
- "Can see ghosts' weaknesses and origins"
- "System turning ghost energy into cultivation points"
- "Ghost fear aura; when afraid, protagonist grows stronger"

**Tropes**:
- Society rebuilding around cultivation and ghost-hunting
- Ranking power levels; top cultivators vs. ancient ghosts
- Ghost types with different abilities/intelligence levels
- Hidden "ghost kings" threatening world
- Government or organizations managing ghost threats

**Appeal**: Power fantasy (system-based growth), world-spanning conflicts, monster hunting, humanity vs. supernaturally powerful threats

### Horror Apocalypse (恐怖末世)
**Core**: Ghost recovery is so violent/overwhelming that human civilization collapses. Ghosts rule; humans are hunted or enslaved.

**Setup Options**:

**Option A: Rebirth/Second Chance**
- Protagonist died in previous timeline (after ghost apocalypse broke out)
- Reborn to days/weeks/years before apocalypse
- Uses future knowledge to hoard resources: spirit paper (冥纸), antique artifacts, incense, spellbooks, cultivation techniques
- Has major advantage when apocalypse hits

**Option B: Pre-apocalypse Preperation**
- Protagonist gets forewarning (from system, ghost ancestor, vision) before apocalypse
- Prepares extensively while others ignore warnings
- When ghosts invade, already equipped to survive and dominate

**Cheat Examples**:
- "See ghost attributes and strength levels"
- "Capture and tame ghosts as pets/helpers"
- "Safe underground bunker system"
- "Resource duplication or infinite storage"

**Appeal**: Survival challenge, preparation satisfaction, power fantasy, dominating in desperate world

### Ghost Hunting/Death Notes (盗墓笔记/九叔电影同人)
**Core**: Fan fiction/derivative works built on classic ghost-fighting IP

**Source Inspirations**:
- **盗墓笔记** (Grave Robbers' Chronicles): Tomb raiding, ancient spirits, mysterious organizations
- **九叔电影** (Uncle Ninth Films): Classic Hong Kong horror-comedy道士 (Daoist priest) movies

**Approaches**:
- Re-imagine classic storylines with new protagonist or settings
- Mix elements (e.g., Daoist priest + modern urban ghost hunting)
- Expand on universe/lore from source material
- Original characters in familiar settings (e.g., rural graves, haunted schools)

## Creative Guidelines

### Core Mystery Design
Every Suspense Brain Hole needs strong mystery hooks:

**Types**:
1. **Origin Mystery**: Why did ghosts/monsters appear? What caused the recovery?
2. **System/Game Origin**: Who created the death game? What's the purpose?
3. **Character Mystery**: Why was protagonist selected? Hidden identity/past?
4. **World-building Mystery**: Ancient civilizations destroyed by ghosts; lost secrets
5. **Personal Mystery**: Protagonist's family/personal connection to supernatural

**Mystery Structure**:
- Plant clues early (easily missed or not understood)
- Build tension over time
- Partial reveals at major plot points
- Full resolution near climax

### Logic & Consistency (Crucial)
Suspense lives or dies on logic:

1. **Rule-based settings** (Rule Horror): If rules are written, follow them consistently. Cheating or breaking rules must have logical explanation.

2. **Ghost abilities**: Define what each ghost can do. Don't suddenly make ghosts omnipotent. Readers need to understand threats to fear them.

3. **Protagonist power consistency**: Don't make cheat abilities suddenly unlimited without cost.

4. **World coherence**: If ghosts have strict death triggers (e.g., "die at sunrise"), apply consistently.

**Procedural writing for puzzle scenes**:
- State problem (e.g., ghost hunting, survival challenge)
- List available information/rules
- Show protagonist's thought process
- Reveal solution step-by-step
- Pay off tension

### Horror Tone vs. Fast-Paced Power Fantasy
Balance depending on subgenre:

- **Death Game / Rule Horror**: High horror tension, focus on puzzle logic and survival. Power exists but often comes at cost or has limits

- **Ghost Recovery / Horror Apocalypse**: More power fantasy; horror is backdrop for protagonist's growth and domination

### Emotional Hooks Beyond Fear
Fear alone is not enough for long engagement:

- **Camaraderie**: Trust issues with other players; betrayal moments
- **Revenge**: Ghost was someone close; protagonist seeks justice for wronged spirits
- **Hope**: Humanity fighting back against impossible odds
- **Cruelty vs. Human Values**: In survival situations, do humans help or hurt each other?

## Platform Notes
- **Fan fiction (同人)**: Allowed but must be clearly derivative; avoid copyright infringement of specific source material
- **Encouraged**: Innovative twists on established tropes, strong logic/puzzle design
- **Avoid**: Unresolved mysteries (readers expect eventual answers), inconsistent rules or abilities
