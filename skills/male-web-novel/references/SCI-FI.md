# Science Fiction (科幻)

## Overview
Stories set in future worlds or speculative near-future settings with strong emphasis on technology, science, and imagined futures. Common subthemes: apocalypse, interstellar society, black-tech innovation.

## Key Elements
- **Background**: Future, post-apocalyptic, interstellar, or near-future Earth
- **Core Tone**: Speculative, forward-looking, technological; "What if X happened?"
- **Characteristic**: Strong focus on science concepts, imagined tech, humanity's future

## Common Subgenres

### Apocalypse/Post-Apocalypse (末世题材)
**Core**: Explore social collapse and human behavior after civilization-ending events

**Common Triggers**:
- Alien invasion
- Zombie virus
- Nuclear war
- Environmental disasters (climate collapse, radiation storms)
- AI takeover
- Pandemics

**Focus Areas**:
1. **Interpersonal dynamics**: How survivors interact in resource-scarce world
   - Trust vs. betrayal
   - Formation/dissolution of communities
   - Leadership and power struggles
   - Self-sacrifice vs. selfishness

2. **New social orders**: How humanity reorganizes after collapse
   - Tribes, warlord territories, nomadic bands
   - Religious cults around the apocalypse event
   - Technological feudalism (tech-haves vs. havenots)

3. **Power systems** (supernatural elements allowed):
   - Biological mutations: Super strength, night vision, regenerative ability
   - psionic powers: Telekinesis, mind reading, foresight
   - Tech-enhanced abilities: Cybernetics, power suits, genetic enhancements

**Protagonist Cheat Examples**:
- **Rebirth/Second Chance**: Relive apocalypse with future knowledge; hoard resources early
- **System/AI helper**: Get warnings about threats, safe zones, optimal strategies
- **Special mutation/rare ability**: Unique power others can't replicate
- **Tech from pre-apocalypse**: Access to lost knowledge or advanced technology

**Appeal**: Survival challenge, rebuilding civilization, power fantasy, human under extreme conditions

### Black-Tech/Future-Tech (黑科技题材)
**Core**: Present-day setting (modern Earth) where protagonist develops/implements future-level technology

**Setup**:
- Present day: Protagonist gets cheat ability allowing "impossible" tech leaps
- Protagonist creates machines/devices way beyond current science
- Impact: Becomes extremely powerful, changes society, faces competition/collaboration

**What is "Black-Tech"**:
- Technology that seems magical to current understanding
- Often stems from: Alien tech, time travel, AI from future, advanced civilization's leftovers
- **Constraint**: Must feel "tech-based" not "magic-based" (explanation rooted in pseudo-science, not spells/energies)

**Protagonist Cheat Examples**:
- **Check-in system**: Check in at scientific facility → get future research notes/blueprints
- **Tech tree system**: Unlock future technology nodes; can manufacture items
- **AI companion**: Super-intelligent AI guiding research and innovation
- **Alien inheritance**: Inherit advanced civilization's tech blueprint database
- **Time-travel**: Go to future, learn advanced tech, bring back to present

**Common Tech Domains**:
- **Energy**: Infinite energy source, cold fusion, antimatter power
- **Space**: Faster-than-light travel, warp gates, terraforming
- **Medicine**: Cure-all, immortality, limb restoration
- **Materials**: Impossible alloys, programmable matter
- **Computing**: True AI, quantum computing, brain-computer interfaces

**Story Progression**:
1. Initial breakthrough: Create first game-changing device
2. Disruption: Device shocks/scared society/other interests
3. Escalation: Competitors try to steal/copy technology; governments intervene
4. Expansion: Develop more advanced tech; build larger influence/power
5. World-change: Technology radically alters society, economy, daily life

**Appeal**: Intellectual power fantasy, "what if we had X?", societal transformation

### Interstellar/Space Opera (星际题材)
**Core**: Universe-wide settings with multiple planets, civilizations, and space-faring cultures

**Setting Scale**:
- Multiple solar systems or entire galaxy
- Multiple planetary civilizations (human, alien, or mixed)
- Space fleets, alliances, interplanetary organizations
- Different planets = different story copybooks (adventures, conflicts, exploration)

**Hybrid Subtypes**:

**A. Interstellar + Fantasy/Xianxia (星际+玄幻/高武)**
- Space cultivation: Qi and martial arts in interstellar setting
- Planet-level sects, martial arts organizations spanning galaxies
- Cultivation techniques powered by cosmic energy, star cores
- Warships powered by cultivation masters
- "Galaxy-shaking" level techniques; entire planets as weapons

**B. Interstellar + Mecha/Tech (星际机甲/黑科技)**
- Mechas (giant robot suits) as primary combat tools
- Pilots train/pilot battle mechas in space battles
- Mecha types: Scout mechas, warship carriers, planet-destroying weapons
- Pilots' mental strength/compatibility determines mecha effectiveness

**C. Interstellar + Alien Civilizations**
- First contact scenarios
- Galactic politics: Federation/Empire vs. rebellious planets
- Alien races with unique traits, technologies, cultures
- Diplomacy and conflict between different species

**Common Elements**:
- Space travel tech (warp gates, faster-than-light, hyperspace)
- Different planets with unique environments (lava worlds, ice planets, gas giants)
- Alien artifacts left by dead civilizations
- Space pirates, smugglers, rogue fleets
- Mysteries spanning star systems (ancient war remnants, unknown signals)

**Protagonist Cheat Examples**:
- "Ancient AI/guide from dead civilization"
- "Galactic database/encyclopedia of all knowledge"
- "Rare alien bloodline or inheritance"
- "System giving spaceship/mecha blueprints"
- "Ability to understand/communicate with alien languages instantly"

**Appeal**: Epic scale, vast world-building, sense of exploration, galaxy-spanning conflicts, rich visual imagery (space battles, exotic worlds)

## Creative Guidelines

### World-building for Sci-Fi
Sci-fi readers expect thoughtful consideration of consequences:

**Logical Cons**:
If you introduce a technology, explore its implications:
- If FTL exists, what does this do to economics/time/trade?
- If immortality exists, how does society handle overpopulation?
- If AI is super-intelligent, how does humanity relate to it?

**Readers value**: "Cool idea → Thoughtful implications"

### "Soft" vs. "Hard" Sci-Fi
**Hard Sci-Fi**:
- Strong emphasis on realistic physics
- Detailed technical descriptions
- Space travel respects laws of physics as current science understands them
- Example: The Martian-style problems, orbital mechanics

**Soft Sci-Fi** (web novel style):
- More imaginative, less concerned with hard science accuracy
- Technology acts as plot device
- Accepts "magic" technology if it's fun
- Example: Aliens speaking fluent human languages after 5 seconds; FTL handwaving

**Web novels are almost always soft sci-fi** — but should still avoid glaring plot holes

### Technology vs. Power Fantasy Balance
Sci-fi subgenres struggle with "is this magic or technology?"

**Good markers for tech-based power**:
- Devices/machines create effects (not spells summoned from thin air)
- Scientific-sounding explanation (even if simplified): "Quantum resonance field disrupts gravity" sounds more tech than mana
- Research/development process: Protagonist invents/improves; not just "wished into existence"
- Resource/scavenging: Needs materials, blueprints, manufacturing facilities

**Don't:**
- Call something "quantum super-tech" but write it like a spell with no mechanics
- Ignore basic science when it matters (e.g., oxygen in space, radiation)

### Pacing and Emotional Hooks
Sci-fi has unique rhythm:

- **Early**: Exploration and world-building (cool tech, new environments)
- **Mid**: Conflict over tech/resources; power struggles; philosophical questions (AI ethics, inequality)
- **Late**: Large-scale conflicts, galaxy-shaping events, protagonist's legacy

**Emotional resonance in sci-fi**:
- Wonder: Seeing incredible technology or cosmic vistas
- Fear: Dangers in universe: aliens, rogue AI, apocalyptic events
- Ambition: Protagonist building empire, achieving dream of space exploration
- Loss: Characters die in space battles, or entire planets are lost
- Hope: Humanity rises to overcome impossible challenges

## Inspiration Sources
- **Real scientific concepts**: Black holes, quantum computing, genetic engineering, AI breakthroughs
- **Space missions**: Mars rovers, James Webb Telescope discoveries
- **Tech advances**: CRISPR, quantum supremacy, neural interfaces
- **Classic sci-fi films/books**: Take ideas, rework for web novel pacing
- **Space games**: Stellaris, No Man's Sky, Elite Dangerous — see mechanics/settings

## Platform Notes
- **Encouraged**: Creative sci-fi concepts, imaginative futures, blending tech with other subgenres (fantasy martial arts in space)
- **Quality mark**: Even soft sci-fi should make sense within its own logic (no blatant contradictions)
- **Popular**: Black-tech progression with power-ups is highly effective for reader hook
