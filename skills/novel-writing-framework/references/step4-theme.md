# Step 4: Theme Development

## Purpose

Extract and articulate the story's thesis, antithesis, and argumentation path, ensuring subsequent beats and scenes serve the thematic purpose coherently.

## Task Instruction

You are a theme strategist. Articulate the story's proposition, counter-argument, and proof path, anchoring key scenes to thematic function.

## Task Details

**Characteristics:**
- Concise and distilled
- Pairable and oppositional (thesis vs. antithesis)
- Verifiable (can be checked against scenes)
- Reciprocally reinforcing (theme ↔ narrative)

**Skills:**
- Thesis statement formulation
- Counter-argument/antithesis setup
- Argument scene anchoring
- Motif/symbol mapping

**Expression:**
- One-sentence verifiability (can be tested: "Does this scene prove the theme?")
- Visual/cinematic grounding (themes must be visible, not just intellectual)

## Output Format

- **Theme Thesis (1 sentence):** What the story ultimately argues or affirms
- **Antithesis / Counter-theme (1 sentence):** The opposing viewpoint the story must confront and defeat/reconcile
- **Argumentation Path (3 steps):**
  1. **Pose:** Initial statement (opening world presents thesis naively or incompletely)
  2. **Refute / Complicate:** Antithesis challenge (midpoint or darkest hour tests thesis)
  3. **Synthesize / Ascend:** Thematic resolution (climax/aftertaste resolves thesis vs. antithesis)
- **Thematic Scene Anchors (3 key scenes, 1 sentence each):** Specific moments where theme is put to the test
- **Motif / Symbol List (3-5 items):** Recurring images, objects, or ideas with assigned meanings

## Query Format

Input required from user:

- **Theme keywords:** [2-3 core concepts, e.g., truth/trust/redemption/control]
- **Symbolism density preference:** [low/medium/high - how densely should motifs recur]

## Theme-Story Feedback Loop

**Theme drives story:**
- Character desires reflect thematic conflict (internal desire wants thesis, external goal pursues it)
- Plot obstacles represent antithesis challenges
- Character arcs embody the thesis-to-antithesis-to-synthesis journey

**Story reveals theme:**
- Scene conflicts test the thesis
- Character choices prove or disprove thematic claims
- Aftertaste resolution validates or complicates the conclusion

**Design goal:** Every scene should in some way engage the thesis or antithesis, even if subtly.

## Motif & Symbol Strategy

**Effective motifs:**
- Recur 3+ times with variation/escalation
- Attach to specific characters or locations for emotional charge
- Carry thematic weight (not just decorative)
- Evolve in meaning as story progresses

**Symbolism types:**
- **Objects:** Weapons, heirlooms, tools associated with characters
- **Settings:** Landscapes, buildings, territories representing states of being
- **Actions:** Repeated gestures or rituals
- **Colors/elements:** Visual or atmospheric motifs

**Placement strategy:**
- Early on: Establish motif (scene shows its meaning)
- Midpoint: Intensify or complicate (motif gains weight/ambiguity)
- Climax/aftertaste: Resolve or subvert (motif's fate echoes theme)

## Usage Notes

- Theme should not be didactic - it's what the story demonstrates, not what it announces
- Keep thesis single and sharp (not multiple competing themes)
- Antithesis should be worthy opposition (weak antithesis makes for weak story)
- Argument path maps to the beat sheet (pose → opening, refute → mid complications, synthesize → climax)
- If scenes feel disconnected, check if they're pulling in different thematic directions
