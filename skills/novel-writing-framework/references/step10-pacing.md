# Step 10: Pacing & Tension Curves

## Purpose

Generate an integrated emotional waveform and tension distribution plan, actionable transition strategies, and parallel storyline coordination, ensuring proper peak-valley balance, sustained suspense, and reading/watching flow.

## Task Instruction

You are the pacing and transition master designer. Output emotional waveforms and tension distribution across the story, transition methods, and parallel narrative coordination for consistent flow.

## Task Details

**Characteristics:**
- Tension relaxation balance (tension goes up and down rhythmically)
- Compact and effective (no wasted scenes)
- Strong rhythm/timing (audience feels deliberate pacing)
- Structure-sensitive (pacing maps to beat sheet)

**Skills:**
- Section pacing classification (slow/medium/fast)
- Emotional waveform design (peak and valley planning)
- Peak placement and threshold setting
- Positive/negative feedback loops (reward/punishment rhythm)
- Information throttling (release/don't release management)
- Parallel narrative weaving (multiple storylines, timing coordination)
- Transition grammar application (how scenes connect)

**Expression:**
- Graphical/sectioned text combined (visualize rhythm)
- Direct to function and effect (state what pacing accomplishes)

**Decision and Evaluation:**
- Peak-valley distribution reasonableness
- Suspense sustainability (does tension flag mid-story?)
- Parallel coordination (do storylines sync properly?)
- Information release rhythm (is reveal timing satisfying?)
- Redundancy compression (cut unnecessary repetition)

**Optional boundaries:**
- Dizziness/flash/stimulation intensity note (for content sensitivity)

**Iterative mechanism:**
- Pacing sheets and edit assumption versioning (note changes/rollback points)

## Pacing Framework

**Pacing states:**
- **Slow:** Observation, reflection, world-building, character interiority
- **Medium:** Dialogue, investigation, preparation, rising action
- **Fast:** Action, chase, crisis, climax, high stakes

**Pacing rhythm:**
- **Escalation:** Start medium → accelerate → peak climax → medium aftermath
- **Oscillation:** Slow ↔ medium ↔ fast (alternating tension/relaxation)
- **Plateau:** Extended medium or slow (build tension slowly through accumulation)

**Avoid:**
- **Mid-story sag:** Pacing dips in Act II and never recovers
- **Early peak:** Peak tension at 20% mark, nothing left for climax
- **Flat line:** Uniform pacing with no dynamics

## Transition Grammar

**Hard cut:** Abrupt scene change (creates discontinuity, good for shocks)
**Dissolve:** Fade to new scene (creates mood, good for time/location shifts)
**Match cut:** Visual or thematic bridge (creates thematic connection)
**Sound bridge:** Audio overlap (links scenes atmospherically)
**Moving camera:** Continuous camera (creates continuity)
**Graphic match:** Geometric or shape linkage (creates symbolic connection)
**Time jump:** Immediate future/past transition (creates compression/expectation)

**Transition function:**
- **Information:** Signal transition of ideas
- **Emotional:** Shift mood or feeling
- **Thematic:** Symbolic continuity/connection
- **Cautional:** Create disruption or discomfort

## Parallel Storyline Coordination

**Timeline management:**
- **Sync convergence:** Storylines meet at key moments (climax, reveals)
- **Anti-sync divergence:** Purposeful separation to create parallel tension
- **Staggering:** Storylines peak at different times (avoid overall sag when all are low)

**Balance ratios:**
- If Storyline A occupies 60%, then Storyline B should be 40% (for 2-line stories)
- Allocate based on audience interest (more interesting = more page time)
- Thematic resonance may justify disproportionate ratios

## Usage Notes

- Pacing maps to structure (beat sheet → pacing sheet should align)
- Tension peaks should correspond to key set pieces (Step 7 high-intensity events)
- Transition grammar supports story type (thriller = abrupt cuts; drama = dissolves)
- Parallel storylines need intersection moments (else stories diverge indefinitely)
- Redundancy labels help cut unnecessary scenes (every line should earn its place)
- Version tracking allows rollback if pacing feels off after test reading/viewing
