# Step 11: Endings & Aftertaste

## Purpose

Design and verify "endings consistent with preferences". Generate two feasible endings and evaluate their emotional and thematic effects, ensuring narrative closure and resonance.

## Task Instruction

You are the final architecture designer. Design and verify endings that align with story preferences, generating multiple options and evaluating thematic/emotional consequences.

## Task Details

**Characteristics:**
- Causal closure (endings feel earned, not arbitrary)
- Strong aftertaste (lingering emotional resonance)
- Completable (storylines close or deliberately leave open)
- Verifiable (endings can be checked against setup/thesis)

**Skills:**
- Power structure consolidation (who ends up where?)
- Character fate accounting (what happens to everyone?)
- Aftermath management (world state post-climax)
- Theme transcendence or irony (ending honors or subverts thesis)
- Open tail design (if sequel room is intended)

**Expression:**
- Cause-consequence clarity (why ending happens this way)
- 150-250 words per ending option

## Ending Design Principles

**Causal closure vs. thematic closure:**

**Causal closure:** Events lead logically to outcomes
- Character choices → consequences
- Setup → payoff (every promise kept)
- Cause-effect chain consistent

**Thematic closure:** Ending answers story's central question
- Thesis tested → resolution
- Character arc completed (growth/demise realized)
- Aftertaste resonates (lives with audience)

**Best endings align both:**

Causal + Thematic = Earned
Causal ≠ Thematic = Confusing or disappointing
No Causal + No Thematic = Unearned and meaningless

**Emotional aftertaste types:**
- **Depressed:** Ending confirms bleak perspective (e.g., "No Escape")
- **Bittersweet:** Mixed emotions (e.g., "Won the war, lost the soul")
- **Liberated:** Relief/release (e.g., "Finally free")
- **Sober:** Calm acceptance (e.g., "This is the world as it is")
- **Satirical:** Irony and subversion (e.g., "What was, is")

**Power structure mapping:**
- **Old order:** Who ruled before?
- **Disruption:** How did story challenge it?
- **New order:** Who rules now (or chaos)?
- **Change cost:** What did it cost? Who paid?

**Open tail design (if sequel space):**
- Hook for next story (mystery left open)
- Character unresolved (new journey beckons)
- World not fully stable (conflict persists)
- But should NOT feel incomplete within this story

## Usage Notes

- Endings should not be arbitrarily happy or sad - they should follow choices
- If ending feels unearned, check if climax stakes were sufficiently established
- Multiple ending options let author choose based on instinct or audience feedback
- Aftertaste should match story's emotional trajectory (don't pivot tone at the very end)
- Power structure reconfiguration should be traceable to earlier conflict escalation
- Open tails can create sequel opportunities but shouldn't create frustration
