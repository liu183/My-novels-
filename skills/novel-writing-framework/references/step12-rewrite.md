# Step 12: Rewrite & Iteration

## Purpose

Integrate feedback and output an executable rewrite plan, progressing from macro-level revisions to micro-level polishing in prioritized stages.

## Task Instruction

You are a rewrite coach. Synthesize feedback, output an actionable rewrite plan prioritizing structural, character, pacing, theme, and dialogue issues in staged approach.

## Task Details

**Characteristics:**
- Problem-focused (identify what's broken, not fix everything at once)
- Decisive取舍 (make tough choices about what to keep, what to cut)
- Layer-by-layer progressive (work from big to small)
- Trackable and reversible (note changes for rollback)

**Skills:**
- Problem localization → solution comparison → prioritization → implementation steps → risk control
- Structural rewrites (whole story level)
- Scene-level revisions (individual problem scenes)
- Line-level polishing (word choice, rhythm)

**Expression:**
- List-format and actionable
- Clear priorities (what must happen vs. could happen)

## Output Format

- **Key Problem Checklist:**
  - **Structure:** [e.g., "Midpoint reversal unclear," "Darkest hour stakes insufficient"]
  - **Characters:** [e.g., "Protagonist's internal desire not visible," "Antagonist motivation weak"]
  - **Pacing:** [e.g., "Act II sags around scene 40-50," "Climax feels rushed"]
  - **Theme:** [e.g., "Thesis tested insufficiently," "Antithesis challenges don't land"]
  - **Dialogue:** [e.g., "Subtext thin throughout," "Character voices indistinct"]

- **Rewrite Strategy (Progressive levels):**
  - **Macro (outline/structure level):** [What global changes are needed?]
  - **Meso (scene level):** [Which scenes need revision or deletion?]
  - **Micro (dialogue/line level):** [What needs polishing at sentence level?]

- **Priorities and Milestones:**
  - **P1 (Critical):** [Must fix or story fails]
  - **P2 (Important):** [Should fix for quality improvement]
  - **P3 (Nice-to-have):** [Optional improvements if time permits]

- **Risk Assessment and Alternative Paths:**
  - **Risk:** [What could go wrong with this rewrite?]
  - **Alternative:** [If this approach fails, what's the backup?]

- **Next Round Delivery Target:** [Con measurable targets, e.g., "Rewrite 5 scenes," "Refine 2000 lines," etc.]

## Query Format

Input required from user:

- **This round feedback points:** [List specific issues from critique, reader feedback, self-assessment]
- **Acceptable change scope:** [Small / Medium / Large - how much overhaul is permitted?]
- **Prioritize these 3 problems:** [Identify the most pressing issues]

## Rewrite Levels Strategy

### Macro-Level (Structure)

**When to use:** Global feedback - "story feels unfocused," "pacing off," "characters don't work"

**Scope:**
- Re-outline key sections (e.g., rewrite Act II beat sheet)
- Delete or merge beats/scenes that don't serve theme or stakes
- Re-allocate screen/page time (shift emphasis to stronger elements)
- Potentially reset Step 2 anchor synopsis if major structural changes

**Tools:**
- Consult Step 2 synopsis for coherence
- Re-reference Step 5 beat sheet for structure
- Check against Step 4 theme - does new structure serve thesis?

### Meso-Level (Scene)

**When to use:** Specific scene problems - "this scene drags," "this dialogue is flat," "this reveal doesn't land"

**Scope:**
- Delete scenes that pass no purpose (re-run 4-scene tests from Step 6)
- Merge low-impact scenes into stronger ones
- Re-objective scenes that lack clear goals
- Expand scenes that need more development (character depth, world-building)

**Tools:**
- Consult Step 6 scene outline
- Reference Step 7 set piece design
- Check pacing mapping from Step 10

### Micro-Level (Line)

**When to use:** Polish and refinement - dialogue stiff, prose clunky, rhythm issues

**Scope:**
- Character voice refinement (make characters sound distinct)
- Dialogue sharpening (increase subtext)
- Prose or narration tightening (remove fluff)
- Word choice optimization (more vivid, precise language)

**Tools:**
- Consult Step 8 dialogue subtext
- Re-check character personalities from Step 3
- Theme-check: does this line reinforce or weaken thesis?

## Prioritization Framework

**P1 (Critical) - Fix or die:**
- Structural breaks causality (events don't connect)
- Character behaves inconsistently with established traits
- Theme is contradicted or undemonstrated
- Ending doesn't feel earned or leaves audience unsatisfied

**P2 (Important) - Should fix:**
- Scenes that drag or feel unnecessary
- Dialogue lacking distinct voice or subtext
- Pacing sags mid-story
- Subplots that don't tie back

**P3 (Optional) - Nice to polish:**
- Minor character personality tweaks
- World-building additions
- Symbol recurrences (additional motif passes)
- Line-level word choice refinement

## Risk Management

**Common rewrite risks:**
- **New problems:** Fix X creates new problem in Y
- **Over-polish:** Endlessly tweaking without improvement
- **Scope creep:** Small rewrite becomes total overhaul

**Mitigation:**
- **Version tracking:** Note changes before making them (allow rollback)
- **Reader checkpoint:** Get feedback after P1 changes before proceeding to P2
- **Timeboxing:** Allocate rewrite hours, stick to schedule
- **Accept "good enough":** Perfection is enemy of done

## Usage Notes

- Rewrite is normal - almost all stories need 2+ passes
- Prioritize P1 problems first - fixing dialogue when structure is broken misallocates effort
- Small scope rewrites may skip macro level (jump straight to scenes)
- Large scope rewrites may require resetting anchor (Step 2) and re-planning entire story
- "Done" is not "perfect" - publishable good enough > perfect but never released
- Track what worked and didn't work inform future projects (build personal rewrite wisdom)
