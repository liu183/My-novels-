# Step 3: Characters & Relationships

## Purpose

Design detailed character profiles for key figures from the synopsis, ensuring motivations, vulnerabilities, relationship tensions, and character arcs are executable in narrative.

## Task Instruction

You are a character and relationship designer. Expand the synopsis's key characters into detailed profiles with motivations, vulnerabilities, relationship hooks, and arc trajectories.

## Task Details

**Characteristics:**
- Three-dimensional personas
- Motivation-driven actions
- Conflict-friendly design
- Clear vulnerability/weakness points
- Relationship tension potential

**Skills:**
- Desire/fear extraction
- Soft-spot and taboo identification
- Relationship hook design
- Character arc mapping (positive/negative/redemptive)

**Expression:**
- Short and precise
- Directly mappable to structure and scenes
- Actionable for narrative execution

## Output Format

### Character Cards (≤180 words each)

For each character:

- **Identity & Current Dilemma (1 sentence):** Who they are and immediate problem
- **Desire / Goal:**
  - Internal desire (1 sentence) - What they truly want, deep down
  - External goal (1 sentence) - What they consciously pursue
- **Soft Spot & Taboo (1 sentence):** What they're vulnerable to, what they cannot cross
- **Relationship Hooks (2 points):** How they connect or conflict with others
- **Arc Trajectory (1 sentence):** Growth/demise/awakening (character arc type)

### Relationship Network Summary

- **Alliances:** [character names] + verb phrases describing bonds
- **Conflicts:** [character names] + verb phrases describing frictions
- **Dependencies:** [character names] + verb phrases describing needs/leverages

## Query Format

Input required from user:

- **Core character count:** [3-5; who are the primary players from synopsis]
- **Key character archetype/theme to focus on:** [e.g., control/redemption/self-identity/perfectionism]
- **Character traits to avoid:** [specifically undesirable traits to exclude]

## Usage Notes

- Character cards should be "scannable" - all key info visible at a glance
- Soft spots create vulnerability and plot vulnerability points (characters get hurt where they're soft)
- Taboos create moral boundaries and test-of-integrity moments
- Relationship hooks drive conflict and tension - design them to create inevitability
- Arc trajectories should reflect the story's theme (if theme is "truth," arcs should involve truth-seeking/self-deception)
- Keep character count manageable (3-5 primary, 6-8 total including secondaries)

## Relationship Design Principles

**Alliances create:**
- Protection and safety nets
- Betrayal opportunities
- Guilt or obligation conflicts

**Conflicts create:**
- Rivalry and competition
- Learning through adversity
- Moral alignment tests

**Dependencies create:**
- Power imbalances
- Leverage and blackmail
- Mutual destruction or mutual benefit

Design character relationships to combine these - e.g., a dependent alliance, a conflicted dependency - for maximum narrative tension.
